Light Up a Comet

Pick a comet, the ice that drives it and how the Sun’s heat spreads over the nucleus. The model of Forecasting comet light curves from sublimation physics computes the share ηX of the absorbed sunlight that goes into sublimation and the magnitude in your aperture. Add your own photometry, fit the coma, compare the ices and read off forecasts.

The empirical law m = m0 + 5 log Δ + 2.5 n log r is fitted (m0 and n) to the same points as the coma. With a date, Fit and Compare use only earlier points; later ones (open circles) test both forecasts. In the paper’s test of twenty comets the model beats the empirical law with fitted n for 11 and loses for 5, and does about as well as n = 3.5; it wins clearly only where an ice switches on within the fitted arc (12P, C/2014 UN271).

Constants of the paper: S⊙ = 1361 W m⁻², Bond albedo AB = 0.01, emissivity ε = 0.9, vapor pressures PX = AX e−BX/T and latent heats LX = BXkB/mX (Table I). K is the Afρ the comet would have at 1 au if all absorbed power went into sublimation; μ follows from K, HN, m⊙ and the aperture. The dust speed is taken ∝ r−1/2. A dust mantle of conductance h = κ/d splits the energy balance into one at the surface, (1−AB)S⊙⟨cos ζ⟩/r² = εσTs⁴ + h(Ts − Ti), and one at the ice front, h(Ts − Ti) = ZX(Ti)LX. Orbits are two-body conics with a low-precision Earth, accurate to a few 0.01 au.
