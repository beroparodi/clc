Exercises for a course: "Forecasting comet light curves from sublimation physics"

The exercises need a spreadsheet or Python, the paper and, for some, the scripts of this folder.
Numbers in brackets are the results to check against.

1. Vapor pressure and latent heat (Sec. II B, Table I).
   (a) From B_X in Table I compute the latent heat L_X = B_X k_B / m_X of water, CO2 and CO.
       [2.84e6, 5.99e5, 2.27e5 J/kg]
   (b) Check the coefficients against measured points: the triple point of water (273.16 K,
       611.7 Pa), of CO (68.1 K, 15.4 kPa), and CO2 sublimating at 1 atm (194.7 K).
       [612 Pa, 17.0 kPa, 105.6 kPa]

2. The energy balance (Eq. 8).
   (a) Solve Eq. (8) for subsolar water ice at 1 au by bisection between 5 and 400 K, and compute
       the share eta (Eq. 10). [204.9 K, 0.93]
   (b) Repeat at 3 au and for CO2 at 10, 16.7 and 19.5 au. [187.8 K, 0.58; eta(CO2) 0.68, 0.29, 0.14]
   (c) Drop the radiation term, then the sublimation term. By how much does each shortcut misjudge
       the CO2 production at 16.7 au? [x3.4 and x17]
   (d) Average over the sunlit hemisphere of a slow rotator, eta_hemi = 2 int_0^1 mu eta(mu) dmu
       (Gauss-Legendre or the trapezoidal rule with fine steps near mu = 0). [CO2, 16.7 au: 0.127]

3. From eta to the activity index (Eqs. 12-13, Fig. 2).
   Compute k = 3/2 - d ln(eta)/d ln r numerically for CO2 at the subsolar point between 19.53 and
   16.70 au, and n = 2 + k. [k = 6.2] Where is n = 3.5 a good approximation for each ice?

4. Afrho from a CCD image (Eqs. 5-6, note on the coma).
   (a) Derive F_C ~ rho for a steady coma from the 1/x surface brightness.
   (b) Take the measurement of C/2014 UN271 of 2024 February (r' = 17.38 in a 6" radius, r = 16.70
       au, Delta = 16.98 au), subtract the nucleus (m_N = 20.79) and compute Afrho with
       m_sun(r') = -26.93. [17 600 cm]
   (c) Measure the comet (any bright comet works) in apertures of 3 to 15 arcsec radius on the same
       image. Does the coma flux grow as rho? COBS data of C/2014 UN271 suggest rho^0.6
       (cobs_un271.py); what does that do to forecasts made in small apertures?

5. A forecast you can test (Eq. 14, Secs. III C and IV).
   (a) Take a comet from the Comet Observation Database (https://cobs.si) with at least a year of
       observations. Fit Eq. (14) (one constant) and Eq. (1) (m0 and n) to the first half of the
       light curve, and compare their forecasts of the second half (forecast_test.py shows how).
   (b) Repeat with Eq. (1) at fixed n = 3.5 and n = 4. When does the physics help?
   (c) Before the next observing season of C/2014 UN271 or C/2025 Y1, write down the forecast of
       each case in Sec. IV and check it against your own photometry afterwards.

6. Criteria (Sec. III).
   For a short-period comet of known radius, compute the largest water production that its
   sunlit side can deliver at perihelion (Eq. 11 with f = 1) and compare with the SWAN rate
   (swan_data.py). Which comets need more than their nucleus surface? [21P, 41P, 45P, 46P, 73P, 103P]
